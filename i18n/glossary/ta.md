# Tamil (ta), தமிழ்

Read `india.md` first. Confidence: **medium**. Machine drafts are decent but swing
between over-literary Tamil and spoken forms, and often transliterate English where
Tamil users expect the native word (or the reverse).

## Script and layout
- Tamil script, left to right. System fonts. Tamil words are long: check buttons.
- Sentence end `.`.
- Audience: India (Tamil Nadu, Puducherry), Sri Lanka, Singapore, Malaysia. Standard
  written Tamil works for all.

## Register
**நீங்கள்** with standard written Tamil (எழுத்துத் தமிழ்), plain and modern, not
literary (செந்தமிழ்) and not spoken forms (பண்ணுங்க, இருக்கு). Spoken Tamil in an app
looks like slang; literary Tamil looks like a government notice. The middle is what
Google/Android Tamil uses. Tamil verbs agree with person; the polite -ங்கள் imperative
("சேமியுங்கள்") is the default for instructions in text.

**Native-terms preference.** Tamil users and Tamil software prefer native words more
than other Indian languages do (அழைப்பு for call, குறுஞ்செய்தி/செய்தி for message).
Use them where Android Tamil does.

## Buttons ("I/my")
"என்" / "எனது" + the plain imperative or a verbal noun works: "Create my account" →
"என் கணக்கை உருவாக்கு" **verify** whether the plain (-உ) imperative or the polite
(-உங்கள்) form is better on buttons; Android Tamil buttons usually use the short form.
First person is gender-free in Tamil ("சேமித்தேன்" = I saved): "I’ve saved them" →
"சேமித்துவிட்டேன்". "I agree" → "ஒப்புக்கொள்கிறேன்". "Not now" → "இப்போது வேண்டாம்".

## Plurals (CLDR)
`one` (exactly 1), `other`. Plural suffix -கள்: "{count} புதிய செய்தி" / "{count} புதிய
செய்திகள்".

## Names and brand
"Tinline", "Osvauld", "DID" in Latin. Tamil fuses case suffixes; on Latin words use a
hyphen: "Tinline-இல்" **verify**, or phrase so the name is the subject. For `{name}`,
avoid fused suffixes: "{name} அழைக்கிறார்" (honorific -ஆர், gender-free) rather than
"{name}-க்கு". Honorific 3rd person (-ஆர்) is the right default for contacts.

## Watch for
Spoken forms, Sanskrit-heavy words where Tamil has its own (e.g. சந்தேசம் for செய்தி),
fused suffixes on placeholders, long buttons.
