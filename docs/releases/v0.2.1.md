# Tinline v0.2.1

Tinline can now rest until someone reaches you. On Android phones with Google Play services, a call or
message wakes the app with a small nudge, so it no longer needs to keep a connection open all day.

## New

- **Wake-ups (Android, Google Play services).** When a contact can't reach your phone, their device asks our
  wake service (`push.osvauld.com`) to nudge it through Google. The nudge carries no message, name or call
  detail; your phone wakes, connects to your contact directly and fetches the message or rings. Each contact
  gets a sealed wake token for each of your devices, and blocking someone switches theirs off.
- **Stay connected all the time.** A setting for the old behaviour: Tinline keeps its own connection open and
  never uses wake-ups. Phones without Google Play services stay connected this way automatically.

## Changed

- The app starts faster: about 0.3 s to the first screen on a Pixel (release build).
- The first message after a wake-up goes out sooner: the wake is sent 3 s into the dial, not after it fails.
- The desktop app quits cleanly from the system and loads block state for all contacts in one go.

## Downloads

- **Android:** `tinline-v0.2.1.apk` (arm64-v8a and x86_64), signed. Check it against `SHA256SUMS`.
- **Linux:** `.deb`, AppImage and tarball, built on Ubuntu 24.04.

## Known limits

- Everyone needs v0.2.1 for wake-ups. Chat and calls with v0.2.0 still work as before, but a v0.2.0 phone
  can't be woken.
- A message reaches a sleeping phone only while the sender's app is still running. If both phones are asleep
  by then, it goes through the next time both are online. A retry for this is designed but not built.
- Desktop and the Liberum Android build have no wake-ups; they stay connected. There is no iOS release.
- Not yet tested on real phones: public groups, idle links and recovery from a QR code.
- No anonymity promise: people you talk to can see your network address. Read the trust model before
  inviting people you don't know.
